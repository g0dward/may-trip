A week in Little Longstone

One quiet base in the heart of the White Peak, the VanMoof and the car at the door, a week of walks, rides, pubs, and the odd drive when somewhere further afield earns it. Wednesday evening dinner is the only fixed point.

Where you're based

Little Longstone sits at the top of the Monsal Trail, ten minutes' walk from the Monsal Head viewpoint, a mile's walk from Great Longstone and its pubs, three miles from Bakewell. The White Peak spreads out in every direction from the doorstep.

Day by day

Each day's plan, what to eat, and the rainy-day pivot if the sky turns.

Day 1 · Sunday 3 May

Drive to Castleton, show caves, valley pub

Wettest day of the week (6.5mm, 100% prob). With the car you can be underground in 25 minutes.

Morning

Slow start, coffee at Michele's. The drizzle sets in around 10am and stays. Drive over to Castleton via Tideswell and Sparrowpit — about 25 minutes through the limestone country, prettier than the A623. Park in the main village car park (paid).

The caves

Four show caves in one village. Don't try to do all four. Pick one big, one small. The strongest pair is Peak Cavern (the Devil's Arse — vast natural mouth, ropewalk demonstrations, an hour-ish) plus Treak Cliff Cavern (the Blue John mineral mine, smaller, the only place in the world it's still extracted, lovely guides). Pre-book online. Speedwell is the boat-tour one if you fancy something different. Blue John Cavern is the working mine higher up the hill.

Lunch

The Castle Hotel or The George in Castleton. Or Three Roofs Café if you just want soup and a sandwich and the warmth. All within five minutes of each other.

Afternoon

If the rain eases (evening forecast says it might): a short walk up to Peveril Castle ruins above the village — twenty minutes up, big view back over Castleton, free if you've got English Heritage, ~£6 if not. Otherwise just drive back via Hope and Bamford to glimpse the valley, home before dark.

Evening

The Packhorse, two minutes from the door. First-night pint in the village you're staying in. Or stay in if the caves and the drive have done you in.

Day 2 · Monday 4 May (bank holiday)

Limestone village loop, then Waitrose

Late start (overslept). Dry all day, 0mm forecast, max 36% precip, 9-13°C, very light wind. ~3hr walk this afternoon, food shop on the way home.

Plan A · Tideswell + Cressbrook Dale + Litton loop

15 min drive west to Tideswell. Park in the village (free or small village car park). Five minutes inside the Cathedral of the Peak — a 14th-century church the size of a small cathedral in a village of 1,800, properly incongruous. Tideswell Bakery for a pasty in the bag.

South down Tideswell Dale, path along the stream through woods, ~1.5 miles, drops into Miller's Dale on the River Wye. East along the Wye through Litton Mill (old cotton-mill hamlet) to Cressbrook Mill — another huge handsome 18th-century mill village, river, weirs, cliffs above, very quiet.

Climb up into Cressbrook Dale itself — the proper drama. Steep limestone walls, wildflowers (orchids, bluebells in May), almost no one. Up Tansley Dale (a side gully) onto the top, across to Litton — the gorgeous village green with the Red Lion. Easy field path back to Tideswell.

Plan B · backup if Tideswell is rammed

30 min drive south to Hartington. Generous pay-and-display car park, rarely fills. Park, do the village green and the famous Cheese Shop (Stilton in the village it's named after, ten minutes well spent).

Walk south out of the village down into Beresford Dale — small, intimate, river-side path along the upper Dove. Continue into Wolfscote Dale — bigger drama, proper limestone cliffs, near-empty even on bank holiday because Dovedale further south hoovers all the crowds. Loop back via the moor or retrace through the dales. ~5-6 miles, 3 hours.

On the way home · Buxton Waitrose

From Tideswell: 15 min west along the A6 to Buxton. From Hartington: 30 min north up the A515. Same Waitrose, just approached from different sides. Drive back via the A6 / B6049 to Little Longstone.

Evening

Local. The Crispin or White Lion in Great Longstone, or The Packhorse if your legs are done.

Day 3 · Tuesday 5 May

Mam Tor and the Great Ridge

Cold start (6°C), max 11°C, dry, light wind. Best weather of the week for a high exposed ridge walk — perfect for the classic Peaks view.

Drive there · post office on the way

25 min north-west to Edale. On the way, swing through Bakewell to drop the return at Bakewell Post Office (Granby Road, ~5 min detour off the A6).

Parking

Primary · Mam Nick NT car park (top of the pass between Castleton and Edale, A6187/Castleton Road). Paid (~£6 all day), drops you 10 min walk from Mam Tor summit — the lazy option, saves the climb up from the valley. Postcode area S33 8WB.

Backup · Edale village car park (by Edale station). Paid, larger, less likely to be full. Means starting from the valley and walking up to Hollins Cross before the ridge — adds ~30 min and ~150m climb but is the proper start. Postcode area S33 7ZA.

Mam Nick fills early on dry days, especially weekends. On a Tuesday it should be fine if you arrive before 11am.

The walk · Mam Tor + Great Ridge

From Edale, climb up to Hollins Cross (the saddle), turn west along the Great Ridge to the Mam Tor summit (517m, paved trig, the most iconic ridge view in the Peaks). Either retrace, drop down to the Mam Nick car park if you started there, or push east along the ridge over Lose Hill and drop into Castleton if you fancy a longer day. ~4-5 miles for the standard loop, ~2-3 hours at your pace, 360° views the whole ridge length.

Gastro lunch

Two strong options. The Bull's Head, Castleton if you finish that side — proper village pub, decent food. Or back in Edale, The Old Nags Head (start of the Pennine Way, walkers' pilgrimage pub, character-rich more than gastro but good food). The Maynard at Grindleford is also drivable on the way home if you want full gastro tier.

Map

Detailed route + turn-by-turn on the dedicated page: mam-tor.html — bookmark this on your phone. AllTrails alternative: search "Mam Tor Great Ridge" but most listings start from Winnats Pass at the bottom (much more climb); ignore those, our plan starts from Mam Nick at the top.

Afternoon · short cycle around Little Longstone

Back from Edale by ~2pm, time for a 1-hour bike pootle since this is your shortest walking day. Up the lane to Monsal Head for the viaduct view from above, down past the viaduct, east along the Monsal Trail to Hassop Station Café for coffee and a brownie (the bookshop is good for ten minutes). Back via the lanes through Hassop, Great Longstone, home. ~5-6 miles, 45-60 min, easy gradient, all village character.

Evening

Easy. The Packhorse or stay in with the Waitrose haul.

The ridge is fully exposed — pack a wind layer. If properly bad, drop off the ridge to Castleton early and pub it. You'll have done Mam Tor and the headline view by then.

Day 4 · Wednesday 6 May

Rest day · yoga + gentle ride + post office

Knee was sore after Tue's Mam Tor walk and you slept in. Pivoted to recovery. Forecast actually dry (0mm, max 8% precip), 9°C, wind dropping through afternoon.

Morning · yoga and physio

Stretches, knee mobility, foam roll, slow start. Warm the knee up before riding.

The ride · Bakewell, Ashford and Hassop loop with PO stop · ~10 mi, ~1.5 hrs

Combines the post office errand with the recreational ride — no need to drive for the PO when you're cycling there anyway. All gentle, lots of villages.

  1. Out from base, down to the Monsal Trail. South to Bakewell on the trail (~3 mi, dead flat gravel, the most knee-friendly possible surface).
  2. Bakewell Post Office, Granby Road — drop the return.
  3. Coffee in Bakewell if you fancy (Aitch's Wine Bar does coffee, riverside cafés).
  4. West via lanes to Ashford-in-the-Water — Sheepwash Bridge, gentle lanes.
  5. Back through Hassop and Pilsley (Chatsworth Farm Shop on the way if you want to grab something).
  6. Home via Great Longstone.

Knee-friendly bike etiquette

Stay seated (no out-of-saddle pushing), assist higher than usual so the legs aren't pushing hard, low gear. This loop has no real climbs, just gentle undulations.

Afternoon · home, rest

Back by 2.30pm. Shower, ice the knee, layer up. Save the energy for dinner.

Day 5 · Thursday 7 May

Chatsworth with [friend] — Pilsley + Edensor walk

Drizzly morning clearing, max 11°C. Re-doing the walk you did last time — Farm Shop ice cream, country lane, sheep fields, Edensor Tea Cottage.

Drive there

15 min drive to Chatsworth.

Parking

Primary · Chatsworth main car park (next to the house, signed off the B6012). Paid, large, fills on bank holidays and warm weekends. Direct access to the parkland and house. Postcode area DE45 1PP.

Backup · Calton Lees car park (south end of the estate, signed off the A6 between Bakewell and Rowsley). Paid, smaller, quieter, NT-managed. Adds about a mile of parkland walking before you reach the house but is rarely full. Postcode area DE4 2NX.

Walk-only alternative · Pilsley Farm Shop car park (free for shoppers/customers, signed off the B6048). If you and the friend just want to do the walk without going inside the house, start here — eat at the farm shop or the tea cottage and you've earned the parking. Postcode area DE45 1UF.

The walk · Chatsworth, Pilsley and Edensor circular

The classic estate loop you did last time. ~5 miles, ~2 hours moving. Through the parkland, up to Pilsley for the Chatsworth Farm Shop and the famous ice cream, along the country lane through sheep fields and dry stone walls, down to Edensor — the model village rebuilt by Paxton for the 6th Duke, every cottage a different style. Coffee or lunch at Edensor Tea Cottage. Back to Chatsworth via the parkland.

AllTrails search: "Chatsworth Pilsley Edensor" — top hit, well-mapped.

Optional add-on · house and/or formal gardens

The walk, parkland, Pilsley and Edensor are all free public access — no tickets needed if that's the day. Only book if you and the friend want to add the house (state rooms, art, ~90 min, ~£24) or the formal walled gardens (cascade, maze, kitchen garden, 2-3 hours, ~£15). House + garden combo ~£28. Decide together, then book at chatsworth.org.

Gastro lunch · The Devonshire Arms, Beeley (or Pilsley)

Two Chatsworth estate gastro pubs to pick from. The Devonshire Arms at Beeley is the bigger, more famous one — about a mile south of Edensor, you can extend the walk slightly to include it. The Devonshire Arms at Pilsley is the smaller estate pub, on your route through the village. Both Packhorse-tier. Lighter walk-day options: Edensor Tea Cottage (mid-walk, walk-in, classic) or the Farm Shop café at Pilsley (cheap and excellent).

Evening

Drive back, easy supper at home or The Packhorse.

Day 6 · Friday 8 May

Bamford + Stanage Edge circular walk

Best weather of the week (max 14°C, dry, light wind). Two iconic gritstone edges in one loop, with the famous Ladybower view from Bamford Edge.

Drive there

25 min north to Bamford.

Parking

Primary · Bamford village pay-and-display (centre of Bamford village, off the A6013 / Main Road). Paid, larger, the safer bet. Walk through the village and up to Bamford Edge from there — adds a short on-road stretch but you'll definitely get a space. Postcode area S33 0BN.

Backup · New Road layby (Bamford Edge) — small free layby on New Road / Hurstclough Lane above Bamford, drops you closest to the edge. Only 6-8 spaces and fills by 9-10am on dry days, so this is the early-bird option, not a reliable plan.

Different-end alternative · Hollin Bank NT car park (Stanage end, off the road from Hathersage to Outseats). Paid, NT. If both Bamford options are full, drive round to this side and walk the loop in reverse — start with Stanage, finish at Bamford Edge. Postcode area S32 1AR.

Free-and-large fallback · Heatherdene car park, Ladybower — free, large, NT, by the Ladybower viaduct. Adds ~1 mile to the start but you'll definitely get a space. Postcode area S33 0AX.

The walk

Short steep climb up to Bamford Edge first — the photographed view down to Ladybower Reservoir is the most-Instagrammed in the Peaks. Traverse along the moor to Stanage Edge (the long gritstone cliff, classic climbing crag), walk the length of it, drop down past Stanage End or via Hollin Bank, return via the moor back to Bamford.

Stats

~8-9 miles. AllTrails lists 4-5 hours; with your pace (you walked Cressbrook Dale at 70% of the estimate) it's nearer 3 hours moving. Add lunch and you're back at the car by mid-afternoon.

Gastro lunch · The Plough Inn, Leadmill (Hathersage)

Best gastro option on the route. About 10 min walk from Hathersage village centre at Leadmill, proper Packhorse-tier food, perfect post-walk lunch stop. Walk-in usually fine. Lighter alternatives: The Scotsman's Pack in Hathersage village (walkers' pub, decent food), The Yorkshire Bridge Inn, Bamford if you finish near the village. Or pack a sandwich and eat on the edge looking down at Ladybower.

Aim for ~7pm so you've got time to shower after the walk.

Day 7 · Saturday 9 May

Bakewell pudding, then home

Dry morning, weather rolls in by afternoon (43% precip, 21 km/h wind). Get on the road by 1pm.

Morning · Bakewell

Pack the car early. Drive (or roll the ebike one last time down the Monsal Trail) to Bakewell. Bakewell pudding — the flaky pastry one, NOT the Mr Kipling tart — at either The Old Original Bakewell Pudding Shop or Bloomers. Buy one to take home. Walk the weir, the sheep-wash bridge at Ashford if you haven't, the Saturday market if it's on.

Bakewell parking

Primary · Granby Road car park (central Bakewell, signed). Paid pay-and-display, closest to both pudding shops and the river. Postcode area DE45 1ES.

Backup · Riverside / Bridge Street car park (by the river just south of the bridge). Paid, often quieter. Postcode area DE45 1HG.

On the way out

Aim to leave by 1pm to dodge the afternoon weather. Optional break-the-journey stops: Haddon Hall (open from 10.30am) or Crich Tramway Village (unexpectedly excellent, 30 min south). Otherwise straight home — about 3 hours via the M1, faster on a Saturday afternoon than the way up.

Walks

Everything within walking range of Little Longstone, ordered easiest to hardest. Grab OS Maps on your phone and download the Peak District offline.

Monsal Head viewpoint

Easy
1 mile round 30 min ~30m climb

Up the lane from the Airbnb, right at the top, five minutes to the viewpoint above the viaduct. Instant payoff. Good sunset spot. Pint at the Stables Bar or the Monsal Head Hotel on the way back.

Ashford-in-the-Water and back

Easy
4 miles round 1h 45 Flat

Down to the Monsal Trail, follow it south past the viaduct, drop down into Ashford. Cross the Sheepwash Bridge (15th century, still in use), tea at the Bulls Head or the Aisseford Tea Rooms. Return the same way. Good gentle leg-stretcher.

Monsal Trail to Bakewell

Easy
6 miles round 2h 30 Flat

The Monsal Trail runs from just below Little Longstone all the way into Bakewell. Old railway, dead flat, gravel surface, perfect for walking. Bakewell at the end for the pudding, market, butchers. Return along the trail or get the 170 bus back.

Lathkill Dale from Over Haddon

Moderate
6-8 miles 3-4 hours ~250m climb

Cycle or bus to Over Haddon, walk down into Lathkill Dale. One of the prettiest limestone valleys in the country and quieter than you'd expect. Mining ruins (Mandale, Bateman's House), clear chalk-stream river, wildflowers in May. Lunch at The Lathkil Hotel before or after.

Longstone Edge

Moderate
5 miles loop 2-3 hours ~200m climb

Straight from the door. Up through Great Longstone, onto the limestone edge, east or west as far as you want to go, home via the fields below. Panoramic views of both Wardlow and Eyam sides. Best at golden hour. Almost nobody up there.

Eyam via Longstone Edge

Moderate
8-10 miles 4-5 hours ~300m climb

Longstone Edge walk extended. Drop into Eyam on the east side, village, museum, lunch. Bus 257 back to Calver (hourly) and walk the last mile, or push on via Stoney Middleton and the valley. Combine with history for a full day out.

Magpie Mine and Ashford loop

Moderate
5-6 miles 2-3 hours ~150m climb

Industrial heritage hike. Magpie Mine is the best-preserved lead mining site in the Peaks, a photogenic ruin on the high plateau near Sheldon. Loop from Ashford-in-the-Water up, around the mine, back down. Pub lunch at the Bulls Head, Ashford.

Rides

All on lanes through villages — your kind of riding. Short pootles first (under 5 mi), longer rides further down. Distances include the return.

Short rides — under 5 miles

Ashford-in-the-Water loop (from the door)

Easy
4-5 miles round ~45 min Mostly flat

Through Great Longstone, drop down to Ashford via the lanes or the Monsal Trail. Sheepwash Bridge (15th century, photogenic), tea at Aisseford Tea Rooms or pub at the Bulls Head. Back via the Monsal Trail or lanes through the fields. The classic local pootle.

Hassop and Pilsley village loop (from the door)

Easy
5 miles round ~45 min Gentle

Through Great Longstone, lanes south to Hassop and Pilsley. Coffee at the Chatsworth Farm Shop in Pilsley or Hassop Station Café. Return via the Monsal Trail or back the way you came. All character, all villages.

Tideswell, Litton and Wardlow loop (drive to start)

Easy
5 miles round ~45 min Hilly enough

Park in Tideswell village (free or pay-and-display). Short loop east to Litton (perfect green, the Red Lion), out to Wardlow (tiny, gritty), back via the lanes. All limestone villages, quiet, just hilly enough to be interesting. Combine with Tideswell Bakery and the Cathedral of the Peak.

Eyam, Foolow and Bretton loop (drive to start)

Moderate
5 miles round ~45 min Some climb

Park in Eyam (museum car park or village). Out west on the lane to Foolow (Bull's Head gastro pub for lunch — properly Packhorse-tier), south to Bretton, back through the fields above Eyam. Gritty country, less-trodden than the limestone-village circuit, plus you get the Eyam plague history if you want to add a museum stop.

Medium rides — around 10 miles

Limestone village ride (from the door)

Moderate
10-12 miles round ~1.5 hours ~400m climb

Through Great Longstone → Wardlow → Tideswell (Cathedral of the Peak, Tideswell Bakery) → south through Litton (the green) → into Cressbrook (mill village in the dale) → home via Monsal Head. All lanes, all limestone villages, the proper Peaks ride.

Bakewell, Ashford and Hassop loop (from the door)

Easy
10 miles round ~1.5 hours Gentle

Through Great Longstone, drop down to Ashford-in-the-Water (Sheepwash Bridge), continue south on lanes into Bakewell for coffee or a Bakewell pudding pickup, return north via Hassop and Pilsley with the Chatsworth Farm Shop. Market town + four villages, gentle gradient.

Hartington southern limestone lanes (drive to start)

Moderate
10 miles round ~1.5 hours Rolling

Drive 30 min south. Park in Hartington (cheese shop village). South on quiet lanes via Pilsbury (medieval motte ruins by the River Dove), as far as Earl Sterndale or wherever the legs want, back. Properly less-trodden country south of the main tourist corridor — almost car-free lanes, big limestone walls. Different feel to anywhere else this week.

Stanton-in-Peak and Youlgreave loop (drive to Bakewell)

Moderate
10-12 miles round ~1.5 hours ~350m climb

Drive 10 min to Bakewell, park at Granby Road. Ride south via lanes to Youlgreave (handsome stone village above the Bradford river), east to Stanton-in-Peak (gritstone-and-stone-walls hilltop village, properly remote-feeling), loop back via Alport. Gritstone country instead of limestone, fresh territory you won't see on any of the walks.

Longer rides (kept for reference)

Monsal Trail end-to-end

Easy
17 miles round 3 hours Flat, gravel

Follow the trail west from Bakewell through four restored railway tunnels to Chee Dale and Blackwell Mill. Each tunnel is long, curved, lit (just), and brilliant on a bike. Coffee at Hassop Station or Blackwell Mill. Return the same way. The perfect bad-weather ride, tunnels are full shelter.

Chatsworth via Baslow

Easy
15-18 miles round 2-3h riding ~300m climb

Quiet back lanes to Hassop, Pilsley (Chatsworth Farm Shop), into the estate from the north gate. Explore grounds, ride home via Edensor and Baslow. Mostly flat, a couple of gentle climbs on the way back. Perfect ebike day out.

Stanage Edge loop

Harder
28-32 miles round 5-6 hours ~800m climb

Out via Baslow and the Curbar/Froggatt edges road (big views from the off), down to Hathersage for lunch, up to Stanage pole and along the Stanage edge road. Return via Bamford, Hope, Castleton if energy, or back over the edges. The ebike does the heavy lifting on the climbs, your job is the descents.

Tideswell and Castleton

Harder
30 miles round 5-6 hours ~900m climb

West through Wardlow to Tideswell (bakery stop essential), north through Miller's Dale and Peak Forest. Drop into Hope Valley from the back. Lunch at Castleton (Peveril Castle, caves). Return over Bradwell and Great Hucklow or via the longer, quieter Abney moors road if you still have battery. The biggest day on the bike.

Hope Valley and Ladybower

Moderate
28 miles round 4-5 hours ~550m climb

Up to Hathersage, then out along the reservoir loop road past Bamford and Ladybower. Ride the full perimeter if you want (adds 10 miles), or up to the viaduct at the head and back. Return same route. Water, forest, dam walls, zero traffic on the reservoir road.

If it rains

The Peaks get wet. Plenty of cover.

Chatsworth House

The obvious wet-day move if you haven't done it yet. State rooms, galleries, café.

Bus 170 from Great Longstone to Baslow, then short walk. ~£28 house + garden, book online.

Haddon Hall

Smaller, older, more atmospheric than Chatsworth. Tudor manor barely touched since 1600.

Bus 170 stops right outside. ~£22 entry. Open from late April.

Eyam Plague Museum

Small but gripping. The village's 1665 decision to quarantine itself, told through objects and letters.

Bus 257 from Calver. ~£5 entry. Closed Mon/Tue.

Buxton: Poole's Cavern & Devonshire Dome

Show cave (natural, lit, guided, kids love it, you will too) plus the huge domed former university building now a café/spa. A whole wet day.

170 bus to Buxton (45 min). Cave ~£13.

Castleton show caves

Four caves in one village: Peak Cavern (the "Devil's Arse"), Speedwell, Treak Cliff (Blue John), Blue John Cavern.

Bus requires change. Easier: eat in Castleton after the Tideswell ride if it's dry, otherwise skip.

Bakewell potter

Indoor market Thursday, butchers, bookshops, pudding shops, the old mill, Rutland Arms. A very quiet-wet-morning kind of day.

15 min ride or bus 170. Zero entry fee.

Safety & signal

Mobile signal
Fine in villages, patchy in the dales (Lathkill especially). Download OS Maps offline before you go.
Emergency
999 / 112. Mountain rescue via 999 (ask for police, then mountain rescue). Chesterfield Royal is the nearest A&E.
Weather
Met Office Peak District forecast. Check each morning. Can change within an hour on the edges.
Daylight
Sunrise ~5:40, sunset ~8:30. Plenty for everything.

Further afield with the car

The bike covers most of the White Peak. The car opens up the bits you'd otherwise skip — gritstone, ridges, big houses, Staffordshire moors. Slot any of these into the flex day or a slow afternoon.

Mam Tor and the Great Ridge

The classic Peaks ridge walk. Drive to Edale or the National Trust Mam Nick car park, walk Mam Tor → Hollins Cross → Lose Hill, drop into Castleton or back. 4-5 miles, 360° views.

25 min drive. Pay-and-display NT car park. Best in clear weather.

The Roaches (Staffordshire)

Gritstone ridge above the Tittesworth reservoir. Less crowded than the Derbyshire edges, more dramatic. Roaches Tea Rooms at the foot of the climb is excellent.

45 min drive via Buxton. Park at Roaches Gate. Half-day walk.

Hardwick Hall

"More glass than wall." Bess of Hardwick's Tudor showpiece, NT, almost over-the-top. Quieter than Chatsworth and arguably more interesting.

40 min drive east. NT free for members, ~£18 otherwise.

Buxton

Spa town with the Devonshire Dome (former hospital, now a café/spa), Pavilion Gardens, Poole's Cavern (show cave), and the new restored Crescent.

25 min drive. Whole rainy-day option, plenty of indoor.

Lyme Park

NT estate at the edge of the Peaks toward Manchester. Deer park, Italianate gardens, the house from the 1995 Pride and Prejudice. Big walking grounds.

50 min drive. Gates from 8am, house 11am-5pm.

Sheffield (only if curious)

Edge of the Peaks. Kelham Island for breweries and food, Yorkshire Sculpture Park is 45 min beyond if you fancy a real day out. Probably one for next time.

50 min drive. Park at Kelham. YSP another 45 min east.

One last thing

The plan is flexible by design. The forecast will move under you again before the week's out — re-check each morning and shuffle without precious. The pubs are close, the bike's charged, the car's parked, and the only thing you absolutely have to do is walk up the lane to Monsal Head one clear evening and look at the view.
