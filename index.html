A week in Little Longstone

One quiet base in the heart of the White Peak, the VanMoof leaning by the door, a week of walks, rides, and good pubs. Seven nights at Michele's, free cancellation in case Warsaw gets in the way.

Where you're based

Little Longstone sits at the top of the Monsal Trail, ten minutes' walk from the Monsal Head viewpoint, a mile's walk from Great Longstone and its pubs, three miles from Bakewell. The White Peak spreads out in every direction from the doorstep.

Day by day

Each day's plan, what to eat, and the rainy-day pivot if the sky turns.

Day 1 · Saturday 2 May

Arrive, orient, sunset at Monsal Head

A gentle landing. No pressure.

Morning / Travel

Train or drive up. If driving, park at Michele's. If train, Cambridge → Sheffield → Grindleford, then ride 20 minutes over to Little Longstone via the back lanes.

Afternoon

Unpack, get the bike charging. Short walk up the lane to Monsal Head. It's ten minutes away and the view of the viaduct framed by the valley is the postcard shot of the Peaks. Grab a pint in the Stables Bar at the Monsal Head Hotel.

Evening

Walk back for dinner at The Packhorse Inn, Little Longstone. It's two minutes from the door, Thornbridge brewery's flagship, well-regarded food. Book ahead for a Saturday.

Stats

Walking~2 miles round trip
CyclingNone needed
EffortEasy
Book aheadThe Packhorse (Sat)

If it rains

Still do Monsal Head, wear a jacket. Five minutes up, five back. Then pub.

Day 2 · Sunday 3 May

Stanage Edge ride and Hathersage

Best ride of the week. Use the high pressure.

The route

Out through Great Longstone and Hassop, cross to Baslow, climb up onto the Curbar / Froggatt edges road, follow the edges north with the valley dropping away to your left. Descend to Hathersage for lunch.

Lunch

The Scotsman's Pack at Hathersage for a proper pub lunch, or David Mellor Cutlery Café up the road in Hathersage if you want lighter and nicer coffee. Outdoor swim at Hathersage Pool if the sun's out and you're feeling bold.

Afternoon

Push up to Stanage pole and along the Stanage edge road (the ebike will thank itself for existing). Drop back down through Bamford and Hathersage Booths, home via the A6187 to Hope and over. Or retrace. Either way you'll hit the Hope Valley's best views.

Evening

Keep it close. The White Lion or Crispin Inn in Great Longstone for a relaxed pint and supper. Short walk home.

Stats

Distance28-32 miles round
Climbing~800m total
Time5-6 hours with stops
DifficultyHard without assist, moderate with
BatteryCharge to 100% overnight, carry charger if possible

If it rains

Swap to the Monsal Trail to Bakewell ride (Day 7's plan). Flat, sheltered, tunnels are great in weather. Bakewell's covered indoor market for lunch.

Day 3 · Monday 4 May (bank holiday)

Lathkill Dale walk and Over Haddon

Honeypot-free on a busy day. The quietest dale in the Peaks.

Morning

Coffee at home, pack a layer. Walk (or ebike, then lock up at Over Haddon) down through Over Haddon into the Lathkill valley. Crystal-clear river, limestone cliffs, often almost empty even on bank holiday because the crowds are queueing for Chatsworth.

The walk

From Over Haddon, drop to the river, walk upstream past the old mining ruins, Mandale engine house and Lathkill Lodge. Turn around at the weir or push on to the top of the dale and loop back via the Monyash road. 6-8 miles depending on how far you go.

Lunch

The Lathkil Hotel in Over Haddon. Hillside pub with a huge view across the valley. Sunday roast hangover menu plus pies, stews, local beer. Book for bank holiday.

Evening

Try The Bulls Head in Ashford-in-the-Water, twenty minutes' walk from base via the trail. Or stay local at The Packhorse if your legs are done.

Stats

Distance6-8 miles
Climbing~250m
Time3-4 hours walking
DifficultyModerate, some uneven ground
Book aheadThe Lathkil Hotel (Bank Hol)

If it rains

Lathkill Dale is fine in drizzle (valley trees shelter). In heavy rain: drive / bus to Buxton, see Poole's Cavern and the Devonshire Dome, lunch at The Old Hall Hotel.

Day 4 · Tuesday 5 May

Chatsworth by ebike

Midweek, bank holiday crowds gone, grounds 1,000 acres.

The ride there

Out through Hassop and Pilsley, in via the estate's north gate. About 7 miles gentle roll. Lock up near the stables.

Chatsworth itself

Three zones: house (state rooms, art, roughly 90 minutes), gardens (cascade, maze, kitchen garden, 2-3 hours easily) and parkland (free, huge, Capability Brown's finest). Pick the house if the weather's wet, the gardens if dry. Pre-book online to skip queues.

Lunch

The Cavendish Restaurant on site is decent if pricey. Better: ride to the Chatsworth Farm Shop Café at Pilsley for excellent sandwiches, pies, and the shop itself is worth twenty minutes. Cheaper and better food.

Afternoon

If you've got energy, ride back via Edensor village (the one Paxton rebuilt), then up to Baslow for a coffee, home along the quiet lanes. 15-18 miles total on the day.

Evening

Easy dinner. The Packhorse or stay in with Co-op supplies.

Stats

Distance15-18 miles ride
Walking3-5 miles in grounds
EntryHouse + garden ~£28, garden-only ~£15
Bookchatsworth.org ahead of arrival

If it rains

Chatsworth is the perfect rainy-day move. House rooms are vast and indoor, farm shop and café are covered. Bus 170 from Great Longstone to Baslow if you don't want to cycle in the wet.

Day 5 · Wednesday 6 May

Eyam via Longstone Edge

Plague history, the best-named museum in England, a good walking ridge.

Morning

Walk from base, up onto Longstone Edge (the limestone ridge north of Great Longstone). Views both ways: Wardlow and Tideswell to the west, Eyam and Stoney Middleton to the east. Descend into Eyam village.

Eyam

The village that sealed itself off in 1665 to contain the plague. Small museum (tiny, under an hour), plague cottages marked along the street, Mompesson's Well where outsiders left food. Surprisingly moving for somewhere so small. Lunch at The Miner's Arms or Eyam Tea Rooms.

Afternoon

Option A: walk back via Stoney Middleton and the trail. Option B: bus 257 from Eyam back to Calver/Baslow (~20 min), walk the last mile. Option C: push on to Hathersage if the legs are good.

Evening

Treat yourself to dinner at Piedaniel's in Bakewell (French bistro, consistently well-reviewed) or The Monsal Head Hotel restaurant. Book.

Climbing~300m
Time4-5 hours including museum

If it rains

Eyam museum is indoor and shelter is plentiful. Consider getting the 257 bus both ways from Calver (10 min) and just doing the village plus a short walk along the church trail.

Day 6 · Thursday 7 May

Flex day. Weather picks the plan.

Decide at breakfast. Two strong options, neither a consolation prize.

If it's dry: Tideswell and Castleton ride

Big day out on the bike. Through Wardlow and Tideswell (one of the prettiest Peak villages, the Cathedral of the Peak church, Tideswell bakery) then north via Miller's Dale and Peak Forest. Drop into the Hope Valley and roll down to Castleton. Lunch there. Return over Bradwell and Great Hucklow or, with legs, up and over the moors via Abney. 30-ish miles, the bigger loop of the week.

If it's drizzly: Monsal Trail tunnel day

Ride the Monsal Trail west end, through the four restored tunnels (Headstone, Cressbrook, Litton, Chee Tor — each spooky and brilliant), to Chee Dale. Lunch at Hobbs Café on Bakewell Station or Blackwell Mill. Tunnels shelter you from any rain. 15 miles gentle return.

If it's properly wet: Haddon Hall

Bus 170 from Great Longstone to Haddon (10 min). Medieval manor, Tudor, almost unchanged for 400 years. Smaller and more intimate than Chatsworth. About two hours. Lunch in Bakewell on the way back.

Evening

Try a new pub you've not done yet. The Three Stags Heads at Wardlow is legendary (basic, pottery, flat-cap locals, very good food), or the Bulls Head at Ashford for something gentler.

Decision tree

Dry + wind OKCastleton ride
DrizzleMonsal Trail tunnels
Heavy rainHaddon Hall + Bakewell
UnsureMet Office at 8am, decide

If it rains mid-ride

The tunnels on the Monsal Trail are a natural shelter. Aim for them. Peak Rail station buildings at Miller's Dale are also open.

Day 7 · Friday 8 May

Easy last day, Bakewell pudding required

No hero mileage. Eat the thing you came to eat.

Morning

Slow start. Roll down the Monsal Trail to Bakewell, about 4 miles of gentle descent. Lock up near the Old Market Hall.

Bakewell

Bakewell pudding (the flaky pastry one, NOT the Mr Kipling tart) at either The Old Original Bakewell Pudding Shop or Bloomers. Argue with yourself about which is better. Walk through the town, the weir, the sheep-wash bridge at Ashford if you haven't, Thursday market if it's on.

Lunch

The Peacock at Rowsley is a 15-min ride south if you want something smart and final. Otherwise Aitch's Wine Bar in Bakewell for something lighter.

Afternoon

Cycle back up the Monsal Trail. Optional detour via Great Longstone for a last pint at The Crispin. Pack.

Evening

Farewell dinner at The Packhorse. First pub, last pub. Book.

Stats

Cycling8-10 miles easy
WalkingJust pottering
BookPackhorse, Peacock
BuyBakewell pudding to take home

If it rains

Bakewell is completely doable in rain. Indoor market, tearooms, the pudding shops. Bus 170 back if you don't want to cycle in it.

Home

Leave early enough for the Saturday train, or roll back by car.

Morning

Pack the bike. Coffee at The Hassop Station Café if you've got time (5 min down the trail, old station building, good coffee and pastries). If the return is by train: Grindleford → Sheffield → Peterborough → Cambridge, give yourself a buffer for the bike reservation.

On the way out

If driving, consider stopping at Haddon Hall (open from 10.30am) or Crich Tramway Village (unexpectedly excellent) to break the journey. Or just straight home.

Walks

Everything within walking range of Little Longstone, ordered easiest to hardest. Grab OS Maps on your phone and download the Peak District offline.

Monsal Head viewpoint

Easy
1 mile round 30 min ~30m climb

Up the lane from the Airbnb, right at the top, five minutes to the viewpoint above the viaduct. Instant payoff. Good sunset spot. Pint at the Stables Bar or the Monsal Head Hotel on the way back.

Ashford-in-the-Water and back

Easy
4 miles round 1h 45 Flat

Down to the Monsal Trail, follow it south past the viaduct, drop down into Ashford. Cross the Sheepwash Bridge (15th century, still in use), tea at the Bulls Head or the Aisseford Tea Rooms. Return the same way. Good gentle leg-stretcher.

Monsal Trail to Bakewell

Easy
6 miles round 2h 30 Flat

The Monsal Trail runs from just below Little Longstone all the way into Bakewell. Old railway, dead flat, gravel surface, perfect for walking. Bakewell at the end for the pudding, market, butchers. Return along the trail or get the 170 bus back.

Lathkill Dale from Over Haddon

Moderate
6-8 miles 3-4 hours ~250m climb

Cycle or bus to Over Haddon, walk down into Lathkill Dale. One of the prettiest limestone valleys in the country and quieter than you'd expect. Mining ruins (Mandale, Bateman's House), clear chalk-stream river, wildflowers in May. Lunch at The Lathkil Hotel before or after.

Longstone Edge

Moderate
5 miles loop 2-3 hours ~200m climb

Straight from the door. Up through Great Longstone, onto the limestone edge, east or west as far as you want to go, home via the fields below. Panoramic views of both Wardlow and Eyam sides. Best at golden hour. Almost nobody up there.

Eyam via Longstone Edge

Moderate
8-10 miles 4-5 hours ~300m climb

Longstone Edge walk extended. Drop into Eyam on the east side, village, museum, lunch. Bus 257 back to Calver (hourly) and walk the last mile, or push on via Stoney Middleton and the valley. Combine with history for a full day out.

Magpie Mine and Ashford loop

Moderate
5-6 miles 2-3 hours ~150m climb

Industrial heritage hike. Magpie Mine is the best-preserved lead mining site in the Peaks, a photogenic ruin on the high plateau near Sheldon. Loop from Ashford-in-the-Water up, around the mine, back down. Pub lunch at the Bulls Head, Ashford.

Rides

All startable from the door, VanMoof-friendly surfaces, assisted-mode climbs. Distances include the return.

Monsal Trail end-to-end

Easy
17 miles round 3 hours Flat, gravel

Follow the trail west from Bakewell through four restored railway tunnels to Chee Dale and Blackwell Mill. Each tunnel is long, curved, lit (just), and brilliant on a bike. Coffee at Hassop Station or Blackwell Mill. Return the same way. The perfect bad-weather ride, tunnels are full shelter.

Chatsworth via Baslow

Easy
15-18 miles round 2-3h riding ~300m climb

Quiet back lanes to Hassop, Pilsley (Chatsworth Farm Shop), into the estate from the north gate. Explore grounds, ride home via Edensor and Baslow. Mostly flat, a couple of gentle climbs on the way back. Perfect ebike day out.

Stanage Edge loop

Harder
28-32 miles round 5-6 hours ~800m climb

Out via Baslow and the Curbar/Froggatt edges road (big views from the off), down to Hathersage for lunch, up to Stanage pole and along the Stanage edge road. Return via Bamford, Hope, Castleton if energy, or back over the edges. The ebike does the heavy lifting on the climbs, your job is the descents.

Tideswell and Castleton

Harder
30 miles round 5-6 hours ~900m climb

West through Wardlow to Tideswell (bakery stop essential), north through Miller's Dale and Peak Forest. Drop into Hope Valley from the back. Lunch at Castleton (Peveril Castle, caves). Return over Bradwell and Great Hucklow or via the longer, quieter Abney moors road if you still have battery. The biggest day on the bike.

Hope Valley and Ladybower

Moderate
28 miles round 4-5 hours ~550m climb

Up to Hathersage, then out along the reservoir loop road past Bamford and Ladybower. Ride the full perimeter if you want (adds 10 miles), or up to the viaduct at the head and back. Return same route. Water, forest, dam walls, zero traffic on the reservoir road.

If it rains

The Peaks get wet. Plenty of cover.

Chatsworth House

The obvious wet-day move if you haven't done it yet. State rooms, galleries, café.

Bus 170 from Great Longstone to Baslow, then short walk. ~£28 house + garden, book online.

Haddon Hall

Smaller, older, more atmospheric than Chatsworth. Tudor manor barely touched since 1600.

Bus 170 stops right outside. ~£22 entry. Open from late April.

Eyam Plague Museum

Small but gripping. The village's 1665 decision to quarantine itself, told through objects and letters.

Bus 257 from Calver. ~£5 entry. Closed Mon/Tue.

Buxton: Poole's Cavern & Devonshire Dome

Show cave (natural, lit, guided, kids love it, you will too) plus the huge domed former university building now a café/spa. A whole wet day.

170 bus to Buxton (45 min). Cave ~£13.

Castleton show caves

Four caves in one village: Peak Cavern (the "Devil's Arse"), Speedwell, Treak Cliff (Blue John), Blue John Cavern.

Bus requires change. Easier: eat in Castleton after the Tideswell ride if it's dry, otherwise skip.

Bakewell potter

Indoor market Thursday, butchers, bookshops, pudding shops, the old mill, Rutland Arms. A very quiet-wet-morning kind of day.

15 min ride or bus 170. Zero entry fee.

Safety & signal

Mobile signal
Fine in villages, patchy in the dales (Lathkill especially). Download OS Maps offline before you go.
Emergency
999 / 112. Mountain rescue via 999 (ask for police, then mountain rescue). Chesterfield Royal is the nearest A&E.
Weather
Met Office Peak District forecast. Check each morning. Can change within an hour on the edges.
Daylight
Sunrise ~5:40, sunset ~8:30. Plenty for everything.

One last thing

You booked the free-cancellation option for a reason. If Warsaw happens, Warsaw happens. If it doesn't, this is a good week. The plan is flexible, the pubs are close, and the only thing you absolutely have to do is walk up the lane to Monsal Head on the first evening and look at the view.
